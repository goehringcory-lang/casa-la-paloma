A letter from the owners

Ten years on the island


We came to Ometepe for an adventure and stayed for a decade. We built Casa La Paloma on the shore of Lake Nicaragua, where the fishing boats slide past at dawn and Volcán Concepción wears a cloud like a hat most mornings. We planted the gardens, hung the hammocks, and filled the kitchen with color.

This was never a vacation rental. It was our home. We helped build school libraries down the road, worked alongside the Puesta del Sol women's cooperative, and learned that a dead end road on a Nicaraguan island is the best address in the world if what you want is birdsong, mangoes falling in the yard, and neighbors who wave.

We are in our seventies now, and grandchildren in California are calling us north. The house is ready for its next chapter, and so is the beach, the plunge pool, the workshop, and the pineapples coming up by the path. We hope whoever buys it loves it the way we have.

Debbie and Ron Goehring Debbie is the author of The Monkey Lady's Gift, a memoir about this house and this island. themonkeyladysgift.com

Good to know

Buying here, and getting here


Foreigners can own property in Nicaragua. Non-citizens hold titled property in their own name, the same as locals. Casa La Paloma has clear title with all documents in order, and we recommend every buyer work with a Nicaraguan attorney to handle the purchase, which is standard practice and surprisingly affordable.

Getting to Ometepe is part of the charm. Ferries run many times daily from San Jorge on the mainland, about an hour across the lake, arriving in Moyogalpa just one kilometer from the house. Managua's international airport is roughly two hours from San Jorge by car.

The setting is authentic island life. The house sits at the end of a quiet dead end road in a friendly local community. It suits a buyer who wants real Nicaragua, not a gated compound: fresh fruit, lake swims, fiber internet, and town close enough to walk to.
